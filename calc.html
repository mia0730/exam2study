Zoning Math

The study guide says: "be able to compute Open Space Ratio and Floor Area Ratio from data on the lot size and building size." This is the one question on the exam that takes real minutes — make it automatic.

The formulas

FAR = Total gross building area ÷ Lot area

Total gross building area = every floor added together. A ratio, not a percent — report it as a decimal like 1.48.

Open space = Lot area − Building footprint − Parking lot
OSR = Open space ÷ Lot area × 100

Reported as a percent. The footprint is one floor — the area the building actually sits on.

Lot Coverage = (Footprint + Parking) ÷ Lot area × 100

Also a percent. Lot Coverage = 100% − OSR — use that as your check every time.

Building height = Number of floors × Height per floor
The mistake that costs people the question

FAR uses all the floors. OSR and lot coverage use only the footprint — but they do include the parking lot.

In the class example: FAR uses 40,000 sf (four floors). Open space subtracts only 10,000 sf (one floor's footprint) — plus the 2,000 sf parking lot. If you subtract 40,000 from the lot area you'll get a negative open space and know instantly you've made this mistake.

Also worth knowing

"Min lot area per dwelling unit 6,000 sf" is a fourth kind of limit that showed up in the example's regulations. It caps how many units you may build: max units = lot area ÷ required area per unit. On the 27,000 sf lot that's 27,000 ÷ 6,000 = 4.5, so 4 dwelling units maximum (you always round down — you can't have half a unit). It wasn't asked in the class problem, but it's in the regulations list, so know what it means.

The professor's worked example

Lot A, Anytown MA — 27,000 sf, 180 ft of frontage on Main St. A developer wants a 4-story condo, 10,000 gross sf per floor, with a 2,000 sf asphalt parking lot. Assume each floor is 10 ft tall.

Zoning requires

Min setback front yard20 ft
Min setback side yard (each side)15 ft
Min setback rear yard25 ft
Max height45 ft
Max FAR3.00
Min OSR50%
Min lot area per dwelling unit6,000 sf

Allowed uses: single and two family detached dwellings, townhouse dwellings, multifamily dwellings (apartment buildings, condos). A condo building is an allowed use. ✓

Step 1 — Height

4 floors × 10 ft = 40 ft    40 ft < 45 ft    ✓ OK

Step 2 — FAR

Total gross bldg area = 4 × 10,000 = 40,000 sf
FAR = 40,000 ÷ 27,000 = 1.48    1.48 < 3.00    ✓ OK

Step 3 — Open Space Ratio

Open space = 27,000 − 10,000 − 2,000 = 15,000 sf
OSR = 15,000 ÷ 27,000 × 100 = 56%    56% > 50%    ✓ OK

Step 4 — Lot coverage (not asked, but know it)

Lot Coverage = (10,000 + 2,000) ÷ 27,000 × 100 = 44%
Check: 100% − 56% = 44% ✓

Answer to the question as asked: yes — the proposed development meets the zoning regulations for maximum height, maximum FAR and minimum OSR.

Note: slide 30 shows this rounded to 45%; the revised solution sheet gives 44%, which is what the division actually produces (44.4%). Go with 44%.

Step 5 — The setback sketch

Part (1) of the problem asks you to show the minimum setback distances on the sketch. You draw a dashed line inside each lot line at the required distance, and the area enclosed is the buildable area:

  • 20 ft in from the front lot line (the Main St. frontage)
  • 15 ft in from each side lot line
  • 25 ft in from the rear lot line

The building footprint must sit entirely within that dashed boundary.
